CODE-SPECIFIC MODULE: Forging Radiographic Inspection

GOVERNING STANDARDS:
  ASTM E1030:  RT of metallic castings and forgings (general)
  ASTM E1032:  RT of weldments (applicable to welded forgings)
  AMS 2175:    Castings — classification and inspection (aerospace forgings)
  AMS 2635:    RT of aerospace forgings and castings
  ASME B16.34: Valves — flanged, threaded, welding end (forged valves)
  Customer prime specs as applicable

DISCONTINUITY TYPES IN FORGINGS:
  - Inclusions (non-metallic): laminar or stringer — parallel to grain flow
  - Hydrogen flaking: internal cracks from dissolved hydrogen — often subsurface
  - Seams: surface-breaking linear discontinuities from original billet defects
  - Laps: folded metal from forging die mismatch
  - Bursts: internal ruptures from forging at incorrect temperature
  - Grain flow anomalies: visible as density variations in RT

CRITICAL ZONES IN FORGINGS:
  - Web/rib intersections (stress concentrations during forging)
  - Flash line removal areas (potential tears at parting plane)
  - Section thickness transitions (fold/lap risk)
  - Die closure areas (incomplete fill potential)
  - Grain flow disruption zones (near holes, pockets, changes in section)

NOTE: UT (ultrasonic testing) is often the primary method for forgings due to better
sensitivity for planar discontinuities. RT is supplementary. Flag this in findings.

SEVERITY MAPPING:
  CRITICAL: Web/rib intersections, parting plane areas, any hydrogen flake indicators
  HIGH:     Section thickness transitions, die fill boundary areas, bored holes through forging body
  MEDIUM:   General forging body — primary inspection zone for inclusions
  LOW:      Machined surface areas (post-machining — RT effectiveness reduced)

GEOMETRY NOTES:
  - Disk: CylinderGeometry(OD/2, OD/2, thickness, 64)
  - Ring: TorusGeometry or CylinderGeometry with large bore (subtract not supported — model as solid)
  - Shaft: CylinderGeometry(OD/2, OD/2, length, 64) — may have steps (multiple cylinder primitives)
  - Block/billet: BoxGeometry(width, height, depth)
  - Complex forgings (connecting rods, crankshafts): multiple BoxGeometry/CylinderGeometry primitives

TECHNIQUE NOTES:
  - Grain flow direction affects defect detectability — specify multiple exposure angles
  - Panoramic technique useful for ring and disk forgings
  - Large forgings: Ir-192 or Co-60 required for adequate penetration
